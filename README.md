Texas In-Store Sales Analysis | Capstone 1

Project Overview

This project analyzes Emporium’s Texas in-store sales performance from 2022 through 2025 as part of Year Up United’s Data Analytics program.

The goal was to evaluate Texas sales performance, identify revenue trends, compare Texas with the South region, and provide data-driven recommendations to help management make better business decisions.

I used MySQL to query and analyze the sales data, Microsoft Excel to create a PivotTable and PivotChart, and GitHub to organize and publish my project.

Tools & Technologies

* MySQL Workbench: Data querying, joins, aggregations, and analysis.
* Microsoft Excel: PivotTables, PivotCharts, and revenue trend analysis.
* GitHub: Version control and project documentation.

Business Questions

The analysis focused on answering these key business questions:

1. What was the total revenue generated by Texas in-store sales, and what period did the data cover?
2. How did Texas revenue change by year and month?
3. How did Texas revenue compare with the overall South region?
4. What were the monthly transaction trends, and what was the average transaction amount by product category?
5. Which Texas stores generated the highest in-store revenue?
6. What recommendations should management consider for the next quarter?

Key Findings

* $3.42 million in total Texas in-store revenue.
* 42.7% of total South region revenue came from Texas.
* 51% revenue growth in Texas during 2025 compared with 2024.
* Technology & Accessories generated approximately 72% of Texas revenue.
* Beaumont was the highest-performing Texas store, generating approximately $637,819 in revenue.
* Texas revenue trends showed a strong relationship between transaction volume and store revenue, with a correlation of approximately 0.98.

Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, I recommend:

1. Prioritize Technology & Accessories: Maintain inventory of high-demand technology products and focus marketing efforts on this category.
2. Increase Average Transaction Value: Explore product bundles and accessory promotions to encourage customers to spend more per transaction.
3. Improve Store Performance: Study high-performing locations such as Beaumont and investigate opportunities to improve lower-performing locations.

Note: Revenue does not equal profit. Management should also consider product margins, operating expenses, and inventory costs before reallocating resources.
